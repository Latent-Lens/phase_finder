The tool-comparison page asks whether we agree with FlowJo and Flowreader. This page asks something stricter: on data where we know the true answer in advance, does PhaseFinder recover it? We built 27 synthetic samples with planted phase fractions — some clean, some deliberately contaminated — and fit each under the full QC ladder. The short version: the G1 fraction comes back essentially exact, QC visibly rescues the contaminated samples, and the hard part (as always) is the S-vs-G2 boundary.

1. What this shows

Each synthetic sample was generated with a known G1 / S / G2 mix, so we can measure the gap between PhaseFinder's Dean-Jett-Fox fit and the true answer — not another tool's opinion. Numbers are mean absolute error in percentage points (pp). This complements Tool-vs-Tool Validation: that page shows we agree with other tools; this one shows we recover reality.

2. The known-answer samples

27 synthetic fixtures spanning a wide range of cell-cycle mixes and data problems:

  • Clean mixes from G1-heavy (70/20/10) to G2-heavy (20/20/60) to S-rich (25/55/20), plus a near-arrested case (48/04/48).
  • Contaminated samples with planted doublets, high-scatter artifacts, sub-G1 debris, and post-G2 junk — the exact things QC is supposed to remove.
  • Stress cases: heavy peak overlap, low counts, clipped domains, drift, and a non-diploid ploidy.

Broken out by phase, the clean-sample error is G1 3.7 / S 9.0 / G2 6.5 (with all QC). The pattern is consistent and worth stating plainly: G1 comes back very accurately; the error concentrates in the S and G2 fractions — the same S/G2 boundary that's fuzzy for every tool. On contaminated samples, adding the full QC stack improves recovery (8.1 → 7.0 pp), and per sample the effect is much larger than that average suggests (Section 5).

5. QC rescuing contaminated data

This is where QC earns its keep. On the deliberately-contaminated samples, gating pulls the fit back toward the truth — you can watch the contamination leave:

Contaminated sampleTruthNo QCAll QCWhat QC fixed
Planted doublets55 / 30 / 1555 / 18 / 2759 / 23 / 18Singlet gate removed doublets inflating G2
High-scatter artifact55 / 30 / 1565 / 19 / 1658 / 21 / 21Cell gate removed the scatter artifact from G1

Sub-G1 debris50 / 30 / 2056 / 18 / 2656 / 18 / 27Structural gate removed low-end debris

The doublet case is the clearest: raw, the doublets (two cells stuck together read as double DNA) pile into G2 and push it to 27% against a true 15%; turn on singlet gating and G2 falls to 18% while S climbs back toward its true 30%. That is precisely the correction QC exists to make.

6. Honest limits

  • A consistent S-low / G2-high lean. Even on perfectly clean data, the Dean-Jett-Fox fit tends to place a little less mass in S and a little more in G2 than the truth. It's a few points, it's systematic, and it's the same lean we see against FlowJo — a real characteristic of where the model draws the S/G2 line, not random noise.
  • Non-diploid samples are not supported. One fixture had a 1.5× (non-diploid) second peak instead of the normal 2×; the fit collapsed its G2 to 0%. Dean-Jett-Fox assumes the G2/M peak sits at twice the G1 DNA content, so genuinely non-diploid populations are out of scope — and this is exactly the kind of case the fit-quality score and warnings are there to flag.
  • The S/G2 split is the ceiling. As on real data, G1 is easy and the S-vs-G2 boundary is where the residual error lives — for every tool, not just this one.

Bottom line: on clean diploid data PhaseFinder recovers the known answer well (G1 essentially exact, S/G2 within a few points on typical mixes), QC measurably rescues contaminated samples, and the honest edges — the S/G2 lean and non-diploid data — are surfaced by the fit-quality score and warnings rather than hidden.
